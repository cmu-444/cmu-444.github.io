# Course Overview

## From Prototype to Production

A successful prototype is only the beginning. Real AI-powered products must deliver value to customers, handle failures, adapt to changing conditions, and remain reliable as they grow.

This course prepares software engineers to turn promising prototypes into **production-grade AI-powered software systems**. You will explore AI engineering from end to end: building the software around a model, deploying it, evaluating whether it works, and keeping it operating and improving over time.

The course spans **traditional machine learning, large language models, and AI agents that take actions in the world**. It examines how familiar software engineering practices must adapt when system behavior is probabilistic, depends on data, and evolves over time.

Because models make mistakes, and agents can act on those mistakes, **managing risk runs throughout the course**, alongside MLOps and responsible AI. The emphasis is on engineering complete systems that people can depend on.

## Official Course Description

This course prepares software engineers to build and operate production-grade systems powered by machine learning. Assuming prior knowledge of software engineering and basic machine learning, it examines how established engineering practices must adapt to probabilistic, data-dependent, and continuously evolving behavior. Students study the full application lifecycle for systems integrating ML models, large language models, and AI agents. Topics include MLOps practices such as pipeline automation, versioning, reproducibility, deployment, monitoring, and drift detection; harness engineering for increasingly autonomous systems, including orchestration, evaluation, guardrails, permissions, and human oversight; and system-level qualities such as safety, security, privacy, fairness, and explainability.

## Practical Learning Experience

Through individual assignments, practical labs, case studies, and an extended team project, students will gain experience with:

- Building and evaluating the complete software system around a model.
- Using production infrastructure for automated testing, deployment, monitoring, and experiment tracking.
- Making pipelines and experiments reproducible.
- Identifying failures and responding to changing data and operating conditions.
- Designing safeguards, permissions, and human oversight for increasingly autonomous systems.
- Reasoning about tradeoffs among quality, latency, operating cost, scalability, safety, security, privacy, fairness, and explainability.

Examples and case studies emphasize real applications with practical and commercial value, going beyond isolated benchmark tasks.

## Extended Team Project: Experience What Breaks in Production

The planned project takes your team through **building, deploying, evaluating, and maintaining a robust, scalable movie recommendation service** under realistic production conditions, with a workload representing **one million active users**.

Your service will run for several weeks. Assessment will consider how it performs under load and responds to changing conditions, as well as the quality of your engineering decisions and evaluation.

You will experience what happens when a model meets production, and learn how to keep the system working.
